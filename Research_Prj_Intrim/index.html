Censorship is not the disappearance of information. It is the negotiation of visibility, a governance mechanism, deployed for protection as often as control.

the precedent study

a library that escaped one censor

Reporters Without Borders rebuilt banned journalism inside Minecraft to dodge state firewalls. This atlas starts here — not to celebrate the escape, but to ask what it escaped into.

The Uncensored Library sits on servers owned by Mojang, owned by Microsoft, bound by their terms of service and their moderation rules. It didn't leave censorship behind. It moved from one protocol, a state firewall, to another, a platform's policy.

That trade is the thesis of this atlas. Visibility is never released into the open. It only ever changes hands, from one gatekeeper to the next. The sixteen cases that follow are sixteen versions of that same handoff with different actors, different rationales, same underlying question: who holds the protocol, and what do they let through it?

The trajectory this project continues, from bound indexes of banned texts to layered software sovereignty.
Index Librorum Prohibitorum

The Catholic Church's banned-books index, 1559–1966 — proof this genre predates the internet by centuries.

The Hays Code

Hollywood's self-imposed production code, 1934–1968 — an industry policing its own visibility before any regulator forced it to. Direct ancestor of Case 14 in the archive below.

Benjamin Bratton — The Stack

Planetary computation forms its own layered sovereignty — Earth, Cloud, City, Address, Interface, User — overriding national jurisdiction.

Reporters Without Borders — Uncensored Library

community of practice

who else is mapping this, old and recent

historical / theoretical
Michel Foucault

Discipline and Punish reframed power as something that operates through visibility and surveillance, not just outright prohibition — the root of reading censorship as infrastructure rather than event.

contemporary practice
Lumen Database

Harvard's public archive of takedown notices — copyright, defamation, court orders — collected for transparency rather than verdicts.

OONI / Citizen Lab

Standardized, volunteer-measured categories of internet censorship worldwide, published as raw measurement.

EFF — Tracking Global Online Censorship

User-reported documentation of platform takedowns, the who/what/why of content removal at platform scale.

Kialo

Structured pro/con argument trees — a different mechanism for the same goal: letting a reader hold both sides without being handed a verdict.

This atlas departs from Uncensored Library rather than extending it — RSF's position becomes one case among sixteen, not the frame.
